When to Replace Your AC vs. Repair It: A Springfield MO Homeowner's Guide

June 12, 2025  ·  Bryan Swanson  ·  6 min read

Your air conditioner just died — again. The repair tech is coming tomorrow, but this time you're wondering: is it finally time to replace the whole thing? It's one of the most common questions Springfield homeowners ask us, and the answer isn't always obvious.

Here's how to think through it clearly.

The 5,000 Rule

This is the simplest starting point: multiply the repair cost by the age of your unit (in years). If the result exceeds $5,000, replacement is usually the smarter financial move.

Example: A $350 repair on a 10-year-old unit = $3,500. Repair makes sense. But a $600 repair on a 14-year-old unit = $8,400. Time to start shopping for a replacement.

Age of the Unit

The average central air conditioner lasts 15–20 years with proper maintenance. If yours is over 15 years old, even a relatively inexpensive repair might just delay the inevitable — and the new problem could hit in the middle of the hottest week of the year.

At 15+ years, we generally recommend budgeting for replacement within the next 1–2 cooling seasons, even if the unit is still running.

Refrigerant Type

If your unit uses R-22 refrigerant (also called Freon), that's a strong signal to replace sooner rather than later. R-22 was phased out in 2020 and is now extremely expensive when it can be found at all. Recharging an R-22 system can cost $500–$1,500 per pound — for a system that's already old and inefficient.

Modern systems use R-410A or the newer R-32/R-454B refrigerants, which are more efficient and widely available.

Efficiency: The Hidden Cost

Older systems are significantly less efficient than modern ones. A 10-year-old 10 SEER unit compared to a new 18 SEER2 unit could cost you $60–$100 more per month in electricity during the summer months. Over 5–7 years, that difference pays for a large chunk of the new system's cost.

We can calculate your potential savings before you decide — just ask.

Repeated Repairs

If you've called for repairs two or three times in the past few years, that pattern usually continues and accelerates. Major components (compressor, condenser coil, evaporator coil) often fail in sequence in an aging system. One expensive repair often signals more on the way.

When Repair Is the Right Call

Not every repair situation points to replacement. If your unit is under 10 years old, under warranty, and the repair is minor (a capacitor, contactors, or a refrigerant top-off), repair absolutely makes sense. A well-maintained system in good condition can keep running efficiently for years more.

Our technicians will always give you an honest assessment. We won't push you toward replacement if repair is the right call for your situation.
