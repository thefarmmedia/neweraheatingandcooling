15 Ways to Cut Your HVAC Energy Bill This Summer

April 18, 2025  ·  Bryan Swanson  ·  6 min read

Missouri summers are expensive if you're not strategic about your HVAC use. Here are 15 things you can do right now to keep cooling costs down without sacrificing comfort.

Thermostat Strategies

  1. Set it to 78°F, not 72°F. Every degree above 72°F saves approximately 3% on your cooling costs. Going from 72 to 78 can save 15–18% on your AC bill.
  2. Use setbacks when away. Raising the temperature to 85°F when you leave for work (and lowering it 30 minutes before you return) saves significant energy with no comfort loss.
  3. Install a smart thermostat. A smart thermostat learns your schedule and optimizes automatically. Rheem's EcoNet thermostat integrates directly with Rheem equipment for maximum efficiency gains.
  4. Keep the thermostat away from heat sources. Sunlight, lamps, or appliances near the thermostat trick it into running longer than necessary.

Reduce Heat Gain

  1. Close south and west-facing blinds during peak afternoon hours. Solar heat gain through windows is enormous — blackout curtains or cellular shades can cut cooling loads by 10–15%.
  2. Cook outdoors or use a microwave. Your oven adds significant heat to the home. Grilling outside or using counter appliances reduces cooling load during evening hours.
  3. Run heat-generating appliances at night. Dishwasher, dryer, and even the oven — if you must use them — are better run in the evening when it's cooler outside and the heat dissipates overnight.
  4. Add attic insulation. An under-insulated attic is like a solar oven above your living space. Missouri homes should have R-38 to R-60 attic insulation. This is one of the best ROI home improvements available.

HVAC Maintenance

  1. Change your filter. A dirty filter forces your AC to work 15–20% harder. If you haven't changed it in 3 months, do it today.
  2. Clean the outdoor condenser unit. Rinse it gently with a garden hose from the inside out to clear debris from the fins. This alone can improve efficiency 5–10%.
  3. Get a professional tune-up. A professional AC tune-up ensures refrigerant levels are correct, coils are clean, and electrical components are operating at peak efficiency.
  4. Seal duct leaks. Leaky ducts lose 25–40% of conditioned air before it reaches living spaces. Sealing them is one of the highest-impact efficiency improvements available.

Supplemental Strategies

  1. Use ceiling fans. Fans allow you to raise the thermostat 4°F with no comfort loss. Make sure blades spin counterclockwise in summer (looking up) to push air down.
  2. Dehumidify. High humidity makes 78°F feel much hotter. A whole-home dehumidifier or even a portable unit can make your home more comfortable at higher temperatures.
  3. Consider an upgrade if your system is 10+ years old. An older 10 SEER unit uses roughly twice the electricity of a modern 18 SEER2 system. The monthly savings often justify accelerating replacement.
