Indoor Air Quality Guide for Springfield MO Homeowners

February 14, 2025  ·  Bryan Swanson  ·  7 min read

The EPA estimates that indoor air is 2–5x more polluted than outdoor air — and Americans spend about 90% of their time indoors. In Missouri, where we seal our homes tight for both summer cooling and winter heating, indoor air quality deserves serious attention.

Common Indoor Air Pollutants

The main culprits in most homes:

  • Biological contaminants: Mold spores, dust mites, pet dander, pollen, bacteria, and viruses. These are the primary triggers for allergies and asthma.
  • Particulate matter: Fine dust, smoke particles, and combustion byproducts. Fine particles (PM2.5) are small enough to enter the bloodstream and cause cardiovascular effects.
  • VOCs (Volatile Organic Compounds): Off-gassing from paints, adhesives, furniture, cleaning products, and air fresheners. Some are known carcinogens.
  • Carbon monoxide: Odorless and deadly. Produced by gas appliances, fireplaces, and attached garages.
  • Humidity imbalance: Too much humidity encourages mold and dust mites; too little causes dry skin, respiratory irritation, and static electricity.

Signs Your Indoor Air Quality Is Poor

  • Frequent allergy symptoms or respiratory irritation at home (but better elsewhere)
  • Visible dust or dusty surfaces shortly after cleaning
  • Musty or stale odors
  • Excessive humidity (condensation on windows, mold growth)
  • Family members with frequent headaches, fatigue, or eye/throat irritation
  • Recent renovation, new flooring, or new furniture

Air Quality Solutions That Work

High-Efficiency Air Filtration

Upgrading to a higher-MERV filter can capture more particles. MERV 11–13 filters catch most dust, mold spores, and some bacteria without significantly restricting airflow. Media filters (4–5" thick) offer high filtration with less frequent replacement. Note: higher MERV ratings restrict airflow more — ask us whether your system can handle the upgrade.

HEPA Air Purifiers

Whole-home HEPA filtration systems (installed in your HVAC) remove 99.97% of particles 0.3 microns and larger — including most viruses, bacteria, mold spores, and allergens. These are the gold standard for allergy and asthma sufferers.

UV Light Systems

Ultraviolet germicidal irradiation (UVGI) lamps installed in your HVAC system kill bacteria, viruses, and mold spores as air passes through. UV systems are particularly effective at preventing mold growth on your evaporator coil — a common problem in Missouri's humid climate.

Whole-Home Dehumidifiers

Missouri summers push indoor humidity above the recommended 30–50% range. Whole-home dehumidifiers (installed in the HVAC system, not portable units) maintain ideal humidity levels throughout the home. This inhibits mold growth, reduces dust mites, and makes your home more comfortable at higher thermostat settings.

Ventilation

Bringing fresh outdoor air in — especially during cooler weather — dilutes indoor pollutants. Energy Recovery Ventilators (ERVs) do this efficiently, bringing in fresh air while recovering most of the energy from exhausted air.
