7 Warning Signs Your Furnace Is About to Fail

September 10, 2025  ·  Bryan Swanson  ·  5 min read

Furnaces rarely fail without warning — if you know what to look for. Catching these signs in fall (before you desperately need heat) gives you time to make a smart decision instead of an emergency one.

1. Age Over 20 Years

Most gas furnaces last 20–30 years. If yours is approaching 20, it's operating in borrowed time. Parts for older furnaces become harder to find and more expensive. Even if it's running fine today, consider budgeting for replacement before it fails on the coldest night of the year.

2. Rising Heating Bills

If your natural gas bills are creeping up year over year — but your usage patterns haven't changed — your furnace is becoming less efficient. Compare this year's bills to 3–5 years ago, accounting for rate changes. Significant efficiency loss often signals internal wear that's not worth repairing.

3. Frequent Repairs in the Past 2 Years

One repair in a decade is normal. Two or three in two years is a pattern. Major components often begin failing in sequence in aging furnaces — if you've replaced the ignitor, flame sensor, and blower motor recently, the heat exchanger or gas valve may be next.

4. Yellow or Flickering Pilot Light

The pilot light or burner flame should be steady and blue. A yellow or orange flame indicates incomplete combustion — often a sign of excess CO production. A flickering flame can indicate a cracked heat exchanger. Both warrant immediate professional inspection.

5. Unusual Noises

Some furnace noise is normal, but these aren't: banging or popping when the furnace cycles on (can indicate dirty burners or delayed ignition), squealing (worn blower belt or bearings), rattling (loose panels or internal components), and humming louder than usual (electrical issues). These noises signal mechanical problems that worsen over time.

6. Uneven Heating or Cold Spots

If some rooms are comfortable while others stay cold, your furnace may be losing its ability to distribute heat effectively. This can be a ductwork issue, but it can also indicate a failing blower motor or a furnace that's simply undersized for your needs.

7. Carbon Monoxide Detector Alerts

This one is serious. CO detector alerts in a home with a gas furnace should be treated as an emergency. A cracked heat exchanger — which becomes more likely in aging furnaces — can leak CO into your home's air. If your CO alarm goes off: evacuate immediately, call 911, and don't re-enter until emergency responders have cleared the home. Then call us for an emergency furnace inspection.
