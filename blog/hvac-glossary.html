HVAC Glossary: Terms Every Homeowner Should Know

January 5, 2025  ·  Bryan Swanson  ·  9 min read

When you're talking with an HVAC contractor, it helps to speak the language. Here are the most important terms, explained in plain English.

Efficiency Ratings

SEER / SEER2 — Seasonal Energy Efficiency Ratio
Measures air conditioning efficiency over a cooling season. Higher = more efficient. Current minimum in Missouri: 14.3 SEER2. Good high-efficiency units: 18–22 SEER2. The "2" version uses updated test conditions that are more realistic than the old SEER.
AFUE — Annual Fuel Utilization Efficiency
Measures furnace efficiency. An 80% AFUE furnace turns 80 cents of every dollar of gas into heat; 20 cents goes up the flue. A 96% AFUE furnace is "high-efficiency." Minimum in Missouri: 80%. High-efficiency: 95–98%.
HSPF / HSPF2 — Heating Seasonal Performance Factor
Measures heat pump heating efficiency. Higher = more efficient. HSPF2 of 8–10 is average for modern heat pumps; 10+ is high-efficiency. The update to HSPF2 reflects more realistic operating conditions.
EER2 — Energy Efficiency Ratio
Point-in-time cooling efficiency (at a specific outdoor temperature), versus SEER which averages across a season. Useful for comparing performance in extreme heat.

Heating and Cooling Basics

BTU — British Thermal Unit
The standard unit of heating or cooling capacity. One BTU is the energy needed to raise one pound of water by 1°F. HVAC systems are rated in BTUs per hour. A typical Springfield home might need 24,000–48,000 BTUs of cooling capacity.
Ton
HVAC capacity is often measured in "tons" — one ton = 12,000 BTUs per hour. A 3-ton AC system outputs 36,000 BTUs of cooling. Most Springfield homes need 2–4 tons.
Load Calculation
A precise engineering calculation (using Manual J methodology) that determines exactly how much heating and cooling capacity your home needs. Proper load calculations prevent oversized or undersized equipment — both of which cause problems.
Heat Exchanger
In a gas furnace, the heat exchanger is a sealed metal barrier that separates combustion gases from the air that circulates into your home. A cracked heat exchanger is a safety hazard, as it can allow carbon monoxide to enter living spaces.
Refrigerant
The chemical that circulates through your AC or heat pump system, changing between liquid and gas states to absorb and release heat. Common types: R-22 (phased out, expensive), R-410A (current standard), R-32 and R-454B (newer, lower-GWP alternatives).

System Components

Air Handler
The indoor unit of a split-system AC or heat pump. Contains the blower, evaporator coil, and air filter. Usually located in a closet, attic, or utility room.
Condenser
The outdoor unit of a split-system AC or heat pump. Contains the compressor and condenser coil. This is where heat is rejected to the outdoor air.
Compressor
The heart of your AC system. Compresses refrigerant to enable the heat exchange cycle. One of the most expensive components to replace — often $1,500–$3,000+ in parts alone.
Evaporator Coil
Located in the air handler or on top of the furnace, this coil absorbs heat from indoor air as refrigerant evaporates inside it. The cold evaporator coil also dehumidifies by condensing moisture from the air.
Capacitor
An electrical component that helps start and run AC motors (compressor, blower, condenser fan). Capacitors are one of the most common AC repairs — typically $75–$200 to replace and fix immediately when the issue is caught early.
Contactor
An electrical relay that turns your outdoor unit's compressor and condenser fan on and off in response to thermostat signals. A burned contactor can cause the outdoor unit to not start or to run continuously.

Air Quality Terms

MERV — Minimum Efficiency Reporting Value
A filter rating from 1–20. Higher = more filtration. Standard pleated filters: MERV 8–11. Hospital-grade: MERV 13+. For homes: MERV 8–11 balances filtration and airflow.
HEPA — High Efficiency Particulate Air
A filter standard that removes 99.97% of particles 0.3 microns and larger. True HEPA filters require specialized HVAC equipment to handle their airflow restriction.
VOC — Volatile Organic Compound
Chemical gases emitted from paints, cleaning products, furniture, and building materials. Some are harmful at high concentrations. UV air purifiers can help neutralize some VOCs.
