AC Running But Not Cooling? Here's What's Wrong

July 8, 2025  ·  Bryan Swanson  ·  5 min read

It's 95 degrees outside, your AC is running, but your house just won't cool down. This is one of the most common emergency calls we get in Springfield summers. Here's how to diagnose the problem — and when to call for help.

Check These First (DIY)

Dirty Air Filter

A severely clogged filter restricts airflow so much that the system can't transfer heat effectively. Pull your filter out right now — if it's gray and clogged, replace it and give the system 30 minutes to recover. This fixes the problem surprisingly often.

Thermostat Settings

Confirm the thermostat is set to COOL (not HEAT or FAN ONLY) and that the set temperature is below the current room temperature. Also confirm the fan is set to AUTO, not ON — if the fan runs continuously even when the compressor isn't running, it can recirculate warm air.

Outdoor Unit Is Running

Go outside and look at your condenser unit. Is the fan spinning? If the outdoor unit isn't running at all while the indoor unit is, you may have a tripped breaker (check your electrical panel) or a failed capacitor — the latter requires a tech.

Ice on the Unit

If you see ice on the outdoor unit or on the refrigerant lines, turn the system OFF and set the fan to ON only. This allows it to thaw (usually 2–3 hours). Ice buildup is typically caused by a dirty filter, blocked airflow, or low refrigerant.

Possible Causes That Need a Tech

Low Refrigerant

Refrigerant doesn't "get used up" — if it's low, there's a leak. Low refrigerant means your system can't absorb enough heat and cooling capacity drops significantly. This requires a licensed technician to find the leak, repair it, and recharge the system to the proper level.

Dirty Evaporator or Condenser Coils

The coils are where heat exchange actually happens. If they're coated with dirt, dust, or debris, they can't transfer heat effectively. Condenser coils can sometimes be gently rinsed with a garden hose; evaporator coils need professional cleaning.

Failing Compressor

The compressor is the heart of your AC system — it's what pressurizes the refrigerant to enable heat transfer. A failing compressor may cause the system to run without effectively cooling. Compressor replacement is expensive — often close to the cost of a new system — so we'll help you evaluate repair vs. replace.

Undersized System

If you've added living space, upgraded insulation, or your system was undersized from the start, it may simply not have the capacity to keep up on extremely hot days. A proper load calculation determines the right size for your home.
