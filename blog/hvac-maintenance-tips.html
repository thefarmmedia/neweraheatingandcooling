10 HVAC Maintenance Tips Every Springfield Homeowner Should Know

May 28, 2025  ·  Bryan Swanson  ·  7 min read

Most homeowners don't think about their HVAC system until something goes wrong. But a little attention throughout the year can prevent the expensive breakdowns, extend system life, and keep your energy bills as low as possible. Here are the 10 most impactful things you can do.

1. Change Your Air Filter

This is the single most important thing a homeowner can do. A dirty filter restricts airflow, forces your system to work harder, and is the #1 cause of preventable HVAC failures. Check yours monthly and change it when it looks gray and clogged. Standard 1" filters: every 1–3 months. Thicker media filters (4–5"): every 6–12 months.

2. Keep the Outdoor Unit Clear

Your condenser unit needs 18–24 inches of clearance on all sides for proper airflow. Keep grass, weeds, and shrubs trimmed back. After storms, check for debris inside the unit. Never stack anything against it or cover it with tarps while it's operating.

3. Clean the Condensate Drain Line

Your AC pulls moisture from the air and drains it through a condensate line. This line can clog with algae over time, causing water to back up and potentially damage your system or create water damage in your home. Pour a cup of white vinegar down the drain line access point in spring to keep it clear.

4. Check and Program Your Thermostat

A programmable or smart thermostat can save you 10–15% on your energy bills by automatically adjusting temperatures when you're away or sleeping. If you haven't set a schedule, now's the time. The DOE recommends 78°F when home, 85°F when away in summer — and 68°F when home, 60°F when away in winter.

5. Seal Leaky Ductwork

The U.S. Department of Energy estimates that typical homes lose 25–40% of conditioned air through leaky ducts. Signs of leaky ducts include rooms that are harder to heat or cool, high energy bills, and dusty vents. Mastic sealant or foil tape can seal small leaks — but call us for a professional duct evaluation if you suspect significant losses.

6. Test Your System Before Peak Season

Don't wait until the first scorching summer day to find out your AC doesn't work. Turn it on in May during cooler weather. Same for heating — run the furnace briefly in September before you need it. Issues discovered before peak season are easier to schedule and often cheaper to fix.

7. Keep Indoor Vents Open and Unobstructed

A common myth is that closing vents in unused rooms saves energy. It doesn't — it creates pressure imbalances that stress your system and can damage the blower motor. Keep all supply vents open and clear of furniture, rugs, and drapes.

8. Install a Carbon Monoxide Detector

If you have a gas furnace, water heater, or any gas appliance, you should have CO detectors on every floor of your home. A cracked heat exchanger in a furnace can leak deadly carbon monoxide. Test detectors monthly and replace them every 5–7 years.

9. Schedule Annual Professional Tune-Ups

DIY maintenance has its limits. Annual professional tune-ups (AC in spring, furnace in fall) include refrigerant checks, electrical testing, heat exchanger inspection, and comprehensive performance verification that you simply can't do yourself. They also keep most manufacturer warranties valid. New ERA's maintenance agreements make it easy — ask us about plans.

10. Know Your System's Age and Keep Records

Know how old your equipment is and keep a folder with service records, warranty documentation, and model/serial numbers. This information is valuable when making repair vs. replace decisions, filing warranty claims, and giving technicians context for diagnosis.
