General HVAC Questions

Most HVAC manufacturers and NATE-certified technicians recommend servicing your air conditioner once a year (in spring) and your furnace once a year (in fall). Bi-annual tune-ups ensure peak efficiency, catch small problems before they become expensive, and keep your manufacturer warranty valid. New ERA offers convenient annual maintenance plans — ask us for details.
A well-maintained central air conditioner typically lasts 15–20 years. A gas furnace can last 20–30 years. Heat pumps average 15–20 years. These lifespans depend heavily on regular maintenance, filter changes, and the quality of the original installation. Systems installed by uncertified contractors or poorly maintained systems often fail much sooner.
It depends on the filter type and household conditions. Standard 1-inch filters: every 1–3 months. Thicker media filters (4–5 inch): every 6–12 months. If you have pets, allergies, or dusty conditions, change filters more frequently. A dirty filter is the #1 cause of HVAC inefficiency and breakdowns — it's the single easiest thing you can do to protect your system.
SEER (Seasonal Energy Efficiency Ratio) measures how efficiently an air conditioner cools your home. A higher SEER rating means less electricity used for the same cooling output. The current minimum SEER2 in Missouri is 14.3. A 16–18 SEER2 unit can save $20–$50/month or more compared to an older 10 SEER unit. Over 10–15 years, that's $2,000–$9,000 in savings.

Replacement & Installation Questions

A common rule is the "5,000 rule": multiply the repair cost by the age of the unit. If the result exceeds $5,000, replacement is usually smarter. Also consider replacing if the unit is over 15 years old, uses R-22 refrigerant (now phased out), or you're facing repeated expensive repairs. Our technicians will always give you an honest recommendation — we won't push replacement if repair is the right call.
A complete AC + gas furnace replacement in Springfield typically ranges from $7,000 to $14,000 installed, depending on home size, equipment tier, and ductwork condition. Comfort & Air Quality Questions

Hot and cold spots are usually caused by ductwork issues (leaks, blockages, poor design), an undersized system, inadequate insulation, or a dirty air filter. In some cases, a zoning system or ductless mini-split can solve the problem. We can perform a load calculation and duct evaluation to pinpoint the cause.
Whole-home air purifiers and UV light systems installed in your HVAC can significantly improve indoor air quality — especially for allergy sufferers, asthma patients, or homes with pets. They remove dust, pollen, mold spores, bacteria, and viruses from the air you breathe. Ask us about our recommended indoor air quality solutions.
The U.S. Department of Energy recommends 78°F when you're home, 85°F when away, and 82°F when sleeping. Every degree above 72°F saves roughly 3% on your cooling costs. A programmable or smart thermostat makes managing these settings effortless — ask us about Rheem's smart thermostat options.
